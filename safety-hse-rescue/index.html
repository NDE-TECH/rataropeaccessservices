Our HSE approach

Safety is designed into the work method

Rope access safety depends on the whole system—not on individual equipment alone.

The team reviews the asset, task, access geometry, site operations, environmental conditions and emergency interfaces. The method statement, risk assessment and rescue plan are then developed for the actual work location.

No schedule, production target or inspection quantity takes priority over a safe access system, competent team, controlled worksite and practical rescue arrangement.
Core control system

Six elements of safe execution

01 / COMPETENCE

Qualified Team

Personnel are selected for both rope access and technical task competence.

  • Role and level verification
  • Trade or NDT qualifications
  • Medical and site requirements
02 / PLANNING

Method & Risk Assessment

Hazards and controls are documented before work begins.

  • Access and anchor review
  • Task and environmental hazards
  • Permit and isolation interfaces
03 / EQUIPMENT

Inspection & Traceability

Access, rescue and task equipment is suitable, identifiable and checked.

04 / WORKSITE

Dropped-Object Control

Exclusion zones, tool tethering and controlled material transfer protect people below.

05 / SUPERVISION

Communication & Control

Briefings, communications and stop-work authority manage changing conditions.

06 / RESCUE

Immediate Readiness

A practical rescue plan and suitable equipment remain available throughout the task.

Rescue planning

Rescue must work from the actual position

The plan reflects the structure, rope path, casualty position, obstructions, available route and client emergency interface.

Identify credible scenarios

Suspension, incapacity, obstruction, damaged system and task-specific hazards.

Select recovery technique

Lowering, raising, hauling, cross-haul or transfer appropriate to the structure.

Assign roles and communication

Rescue leader, rescuers, first aid and client emergency contacts.

Stage and verify equipment

Suitable rescue equipment is available and ready for immediate use.

Brief and maintain readiness

The plan is reviewed before work and reassessed when conditions change.

Stop-work responsibility

Changed conditions require reassessment

When work stops

Unsafe anchors, damaged equipment, uncontrolled simultaneous operations, lost communication, weather change or any condition outside the approved method.

How work restarts

The supervisor reviews the change, updates controls, confirms rescue readiness and re-briefs the team.

Important: Final HSE documentation and certification requirements are developed for the specific project, jurisdiction, site and approved scope.
